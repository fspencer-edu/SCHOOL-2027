## The Blood-Brain Barrier

- Many chemical cannot cross from the blood to the brain

### Why We Need a Blood-Brain Barrier

- Vertebrate brain cannot replace damaged neurons
- Brain's blood vessels are tightly packed cells that keep out most viruses and bacteria
- Brain's microglia attack most viruses that enter the nervous system
	- Inflammatory response
	- Fights virus without killing the neuron
	- Controls without killing it
- Spirochete responsible for syphilis can penetrate the blood-brain barrier
	- Long lasting and fatal consequences
- Chickenpox
	- Enters spinal cord cells
	- Virus particles remain
- Genital  herpes
	- Hides in the nervous system
- COVID-19 virus crosses the blood-brain barrier in mice
	- Can harm the brain by constricting capillaries, and decreasing blood flow to the brain
	- Triggers an immune response

### How the Blood-Brain Barrier Works

- Blood-brain barrier depends on the endothelial cells that form the walls of the capillaries
- In the brain, capillaries are joined tightly that they block almost anything from passage
- Most large molecules and electrically charged molecules cannot cross from the blood to the brain
- Fat soluble molecules can cross easily
	- Oxygen
	- Carbon dioxide
- Narrow channels
	- Water, sodium, potassium, and chloride
- Active transport systems pump
	- Glucose
	- Amino acids
	- Vitamines

## The Resting Potential of the Neuron

- Resting potiential
	- Difference in electrical charge between the inside and outside of a neuron

- Resting potential
	- Difference in voltage

### Forces Acting on Sodium and Potassium Ions

- Membrane is selectively permeable
	- Lets some chemicals pass

	- Oxygen, carbon dioxide, urea, water
- Charged ions
	- Sodium, potassium, calcium, chloride
- Sodium and potassium channels are closed at rest

	- Transports 3 sodium ions out of the cell while drawing two potassium ions in

- Concentration gradient

	- Potassium is more concentrate inside, so tends to drive out

### The All-or-None Law

- Any depolarization that reaches or passes the threshold produces an action potential
- All action potentials are approximately equal in amplitude (intensity) and velocity
- Amplitude and velocity are independent of the intensity of the stimulus initiated
- Signal difference between a weak and strong stimulus
	- Frequency of its action potentials

### The Molecular Basis of the Action Potential

1) At rest, sodium ions are outside, and potassium ions inside
2) Depolarizing the membrane opens the sodium and potassium channels
3) At peak of action potential, sodium channels close

- Voltage gated channels
	- Open or close depending on the voltage across the membrane
	- Sodium channel
	- Potassium channel
- At rest both sodium and potassium channels are closed
- During depolarization the channels open
- When sodium enter, electrical potential passes beyond zero to a reversed polarity
	- Less than 1% of sodium ions cross the membrane during action potential
	- Still more concentrated outside the cell

## Propagation of the Action Potential

- Positive charge flows to neighbouring regions of the axon

### The Refractory Period

- At the peak of the action potential, the sodium channels shut for the next ms

- Relative refractory period

## Local Neurons

- Local neurons
	- Tiny neuron
	- Have no axon and communicate only with their immediate neighbours
	- Do not produce action potential
	- Do not follow all-or-none law
	- Greater amounts of stimulation produce greater depolarization (graded potential)
	- Spreads over the surface of tiny neuron, declining in strength over distance
	- Inhibitory effects on neighbouring cells
