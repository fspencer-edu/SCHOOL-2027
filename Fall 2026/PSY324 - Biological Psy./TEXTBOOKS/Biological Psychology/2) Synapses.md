# The Concepts of the Synapse

- Charles Scott Sherrington
	- Discovered the synapse
	- Demonstrated that neurons communicate at a junction between them
	- Used behavioural observations to infer the properties of synapses

## Properties of Synapses

- Reflexes
	- Automatic muscular responses to stimuli
- A sensory neuron excites a second neuron, which it turn excites a motor neuron, which excites a muscle 
- Reflex arc
	- Circuit from sensory neuron to muscle response

- Spinal cord controlled the flexion and extension reflexes
- Movements were more consistent after he separated the spinal cord from the brain
- Messages descend from the brain modify the reflexes, making them stronger at some times and weaker at others

1) Reflexes are slowed then conducting along an axon
2) Several weak stimuli presented at nearby places or times combine their effects
3) When one set of muscles become excited, a different set becomes relaxed

### Speed of a Reflex and Delayed Transmission at the Synapse

- Speed of conduction through the reflex arc varied
	- Never more than 15 m/s
- Action potential was measured along sensory or motor nerves at about 40 m/s
- Some process must be slowing conduction through the reflex
- Delayed occurs where one neuron communicates with another

### Temporal Summation

- Repeated stimuli within a brief time combine their effects
- Temporal summation
- Presynaptic neuron = neuron delivering transmission
- Postsynaptic neuron = neuron receiving transmission
- John Eccles
	- Attached micro electrodes to stimulate axons or presynaptic neurons and recorded from postsynaptic enurons
	- Slight depolarization of the membrane of the postsynaptic cell or a slight hyper polarization

- Graded potential

- Excitatory postsynaptic potential (EPSP)
	- Graded depolarization
- Inhibitory postsynaptic potential (IPSP)
	- Graded hyper-polarization
	- Negatively charged chloride ions into the cell

- Summation over space
- Two pinching points activated sensory neurons whose axons converged onto a neuron in the spinal cord

# Chemical Events at the Synapse

- Charles Sherrington though it was still too fast to depend on a chemical process, and concluded that it was electrical
- Majority of synapses rely on chemical processes
	- Faster and more versatile

## The Discovery of Chemical Transmissions at Synapses

- Sympathetic nervous system
	- Heartbeat
	- Dilates pupils
	- Regulates other organs
- T.R. Elliott
	- Discovered that applying adrenaline directly to the surface of the heart, stomach, or the pupils produces the same effects
- Sympathetic nerve stimulate muscles by releasing adrenaline or a similar chemical

- Otto Loewi
	- Stimulated a frog's vagus nerve
	- The fluid decreased the second frog's heart rate
- Stimulating one nerve releasing something that inhibited heart rate, and stimulating a different nerve released something that increased it
- Nerves send messages by releasing chemicals

## The Chemical Events at the Synapse

1) Neuron synthesizes chemical that serve as neurotransmitters, either in the cell body or at the end of the axon
2) Action potentials travel down the axon
	1) At presynaptic terminal, depolarization enables calcium to enter the cell
	2) Calcium releases neurotransmitters from the terminal and into the synaptic cleft
3) The released molecules diffuse across the narrow cleft, attach to receptors, and alter the activity of the postsynaptic neuron
4) Neurotransmitter molecules separate from their receptors
5) Neurotransmitters molecules may be taken back into the presynaptic neuron for recycling, or they may diffuse
6) Some postsynaptic cells send reverse messages to control the further release of neurotransmitters by presynaptic cells

### Release of Transmitters

- An action potential does not release neurotransmitters
- Depolarization opens voltage-dependent calcium channels in the presynaptic terminal
- Exocytosis
	- Burst of release of neurotransmitters from the presynaptic neuron
- Attaches to postsynaptic receptors
- 0.01 ms to diffuse across cleft
- 20 to 30 nm wide
- Neurons realize a combination of two more transmitters

1) Na+ enters (depolarization)
2) K+ leaves (repolarization)
3) Action potential reaches the axon terminal
4) Ca2+ enters the presynaptic terminal
	1) Causes neurotransmitter release through exocytosis
5) Neurotransmitter crosses the synaptic cleft
6) Receptors can change Na+ or K+ movement in the next neurons

### Effects on the Postsynaptic Cell

- Effects of a neurotransmitter depends on how it affects it receptor
- receptor may open a channel (ionotropic) or produce a slower but longer effect (metabotropic)

#### Ionotropic Effects

- When a neurotransmitter binds to an ionotropic receptor, it twists the receptor to open its central channel
- Controlled by a transmitted-gated or ligand-gated channel
	- Ligand is a chemical that binds to something
- Glutamate
	- Most abundant neurotransmitter in the nervous system
	- Opens sodium channels
- GABA (gamma-aminobutyric acid)
	- Opens chloride channel
- Glycine
	- Spinal cord
- Acetylcholine
	- Outer portion is embedded in the neuron's membrane
	- Inner portion surrounds the sodium channel
- Localized effects to one point on the membrane

#### Metabotropic Effects and Second Messenger Systems

- Initiating a sequence of metabolic reactions that start more slowly but last longer than ionotropic effects
- Neuromodulators
- 100 ms or more
- Some neuropeptides exert effects that last 20 mins+
- G protein
	- A protein coupled to guanosine triphosphate (GTP), an energy storing molecule
- Bending the receptor protein detaches the G protein, which is then free to take its energy somewhere else
- G protein is increased concentration of a second messenger
- Influences activity in much or all of the cell over a longer time

### Inactivation and Reuptake of Neurotransmitters

- Reuptake
	- Specialized transporter proteins move molecules back into the presynaptic cell
	- Recycling
	- Half the effect on the postsynaptic cell
- Acetylcholine
	- Undergoes reuptake after the enzyme breaks into two fragments
		- Acetate
		- Choline
- Rapid series of action potential at a synapse can deplete the neurotransmitter faster than the presynaptic cell replenishes it, impairing transmission
- Enzymes break down any transmitter molecules that the transporters do not reuptake
- Breakdown products wash away and end up in blood and urine
- Neuropeptides
	- Diffuse without uptake
- Amphetamine and cocaine
	- Inhibit the transporters for dopamine, serotonin, and norepinephrine
	- Decreases reuptake and prologs the effects of the neurotransmitters
	- Increased arousal
- A few hours after taking a stimulant drug, a user has less than usual dopamine and enters a withdrawal state
	- Reduced energy, motivation and mild depression
	- Enzymes break down extra dopamine in the synaptic cleft
- Methylphenidate (Ritalin)
	- Deficit/hyperactivity disorder
	- Blocks the reuptake of dopamine
	- More gradual effects
### Negative Feedback from the Postsynaptic Cell

- Many presynaptic terminals have receptors sensitive to the same transmitter they release
- Autoreceptors
	- Respond to the released transmitter by inhibiting further synthesis and release
	- Negative feedback
- Some postsynaptic neurons respond to stimulation by releasing chemicals that travel back to the presynaptic terminal to inhibit further release of the transmitter
	- Anandamide
	- 2-AG (sn-2 arachidonylglycerol)
-  Cannabinoids
	- Active chemicals in marijuana
	- Mimic effects of the reverse transmitters
	- Decrease the release of both glutamate and GABA
	- Decrease anxiety
