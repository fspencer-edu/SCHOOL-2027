## Sherrington's Observations

1) Reflexes are slower than conduction along an axon
2) Several weak stimuli present at slightly different times or location produce a stronger reflex than a single stimulus
3) As one set of muscles becomes excited, another set relaxes

### 1 - Reflexes are slowed than conduction along an axon

- An impulse traveling through a synapse in the spinal cord is slower than one traveling a similar distance along an uninterrupted axon
	- 40 m/s
- The speed of conduction through a reflex arc is slower and more variable
- Delay occurs at the synapse
- Sherrington found a difference in the speed of conduction in a reflex arc from previously measured action potential
- Differences must be accounted by the time it took for communication between neurons
- This evidence validated the idea of the synapse

### 2 - Several weak stimuli present at slightly different times or locations produce a stronger reflex than a single stimulus

- Grade potentials
	- Temporary changes in the membrane voltage
	- Depend on the size of the stimulus
- A single graded potential does not lead to an action potential, but they can combine
- Temporal summation
- Spatial summation

- Repeated stimuli in the same location in rapid succession
- Stimuli in several close locations at one time

### 3 - As one set of muscles becomes excited, another set relaxes

- Excitatory synapses
	- Make neuron more likely to fire
- Inhibitory synapses
	- Make neuron less likely to fire

- When a flexor muscle is excited, input to the extensor muscle is inhibited
- Inferred that the interneuron that excited a motor neuron to the flexor muscle also inhibited a motor neuron connected to the extensor muscle

## Excitatory and Inhibitory Neurons

- Excitatory postsynaptic potential (EPSP)
	- A graded depolarization
	- Caused by positively charged sodium entering the neuron
- Inhibitory postsynaptic potential (IPSP)
	- A graded hyperpolarization
	- Caused by negatively charged chloride ions entering the neuron

# Synapses

1) Action potential arrives at axon terminal
2) Voltage gated Ca2+ channels open and Ca2+ enters the axon terminal
3) Ca2+ entry causes neurotransmitter-containing synaptic vesicles to released contents by exocytosis
4) Neurotransmitter diffuses across the synaptic cleft and binds to ligand-gated ion channels on the postsynaptic membrane
5) Binding of neurotransmitter opens ligand-gated ion channels, resulting in graded potentials
6) Reuptake by the presynaptic neuron, enzymatic degradation and diffusion reduce neurotransmitter levels, terminating the signal

## What is a neurotransmitter?

1) Chemicals produced within a neuron
2) Released by a a neuron following depolarization from presynaptic neuron and acts on adjacent post synaptic neuron
3) Exogenous administration mimics endogenous release
4) Does not remain in the postsynaptic cleft for a long period (clearance)

# How do neurotransmitters open ion channels?

- Ionotropic receptor
	- A receptor that contains a binding site for a neurotransmitter and an ion channel
	- Opens when a molecule of the neurotransmitter attaches to the binding site
	- Fast acting
	- Most excitatory ionotropic synapses, use glutamate
		- Opens Na+
	- Most inhibitory ionotropic synapses GABA (gamma-aminobutyric acid)
		- Opens Cl-
- Cross section of the receptor at rest
- Similar view after acetylcholine has attached to the side of the receptor, opening the central channel wide enough for sodium to pass through

- Metabotropic
	- Receptor that contains a binding site for a neurotransmitter which then activates an enzyme that beings a series of events that opens an ion channel elsewhere in the membrane of the cell
	- Slow, but amplifed
	- Last longer
	- Can be both inhibitory and excitatory, depending on
		- Type of receptor
		- G-protein they activate
		- Downstream pathways involved
	- Receptor bends from attached neurotransmitter, and releases G protein
	- G protein activates a second messenger such as cyclic AMP, which alters a metabolic pathway, turns on a gene in the nucleus, or opens or loses an ion channel
