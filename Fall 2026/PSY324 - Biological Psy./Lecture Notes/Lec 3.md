## Sherrington's Observations

1) Reflexes are slower than conduction along an axon
2) Several weak stimuli present at slightly different times or location produce a stronger reflex than a single stimulus
3) As one set of muscles becomes excited, another set relaxes

### 1 - Reflexes are slowed than conduction along an axon

- An impulse traveling through a synapse in the spinal cord is slower than one traveling a similar distance along an uninterrupted axon
	- 40 m/s
- The speed of conduction through a reflex arc is slower and more variable
- Delay occurs at the synapse
- Sherrington found a difference in the speed of conduction in a reflex arc from previously measured action potential
- Differences must be accounted by the time it took for communication between neurons
- This evidence validated the idea of the synapse

### 2 - Several weak stimuli present at slightly different times or locations produce a stronger reflex than a single stimulus

- Grade potentials
	- Temporary changes in the membrane voltage
	- Depend on the size of the stimulus
- A single graded potential does not lead to an action potential, but they can combine
- Temporal summation
- Spatial summation

- Repeated stimuli in the same location in rapid succession
- Stimuli in several close locations at one time

### 3 - As one set of muscles becomes excited, another set relaxes

- Excitatory synapses
	- Make neuron more likely to fire
- Inhibitory synapses
	- Make neuron less likely to fire

- When a flexor muscle is excited, input to the extensor muscle is inhibited
- Inferred that the interneuron that excited a motor neuron to the flexor muscle also inhibited a motor neuron connected to the extensor muscle

## Excitatory and Inhibitory Neurons

- Excitatory postsynaptic potential (EPSP)
	- A graded depolarization
	- Caused by positively charged sodium entering the neuron
- Inhibitory postsynaptic potential (IPSP)
	- A graded hyperpolarization
	- Caused by negatively charged chloride ions entering the neuron
