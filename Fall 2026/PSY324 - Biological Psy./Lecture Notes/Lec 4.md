# The Peripheral Nervous System (PNS)

- The brain and spinal cord communicate with the rest of the body via the cranial nerves and the spinal nerves
- 31 pairs of spinal nerves that originate in the spinal cord
- 12 pairs of cranial nerves that originate in the brain

- Somatic nervous system
	- Controls movement of skeletal muscles or transmits somatosensory information to the CNS from the skin and sense organs
	- Cranial nerves and spinal nerves
- Autonomic
	- Portion of the PNS that conrols the bodies vegetative function s(smooth muscle, cardiac muscle, and glands)
		- Sympathetic
		- Parasympathetic

- Spinal nerves
	- From the vertebral column, fibers travel to muscles or from sensory receptors
	- Doral root
		- Allow sensory neurons to enter spinal cord
			- Afferent axons
		- Ventral root
			- Allow motor neurons to ext spinal cord
				- Efferent axons

## PNS - Autonomic Nervous System

		- Heart rate
		- Epinephrine release

		- Short neurons that synapse in sympathetic ganglia near spinal cord

	- Processes associated with conserving and restoring energy

		- Blood flow to gastrointestinal tract
		- Digestion
	- Preganglonic
		- Long neurons from cranial nerve region
	- Postganglionic
		- Short neurons located close to or in target organs
	- Rest and digest (restoration and conservation)

# The Brain

- 86 B neurons
- 10^14 connections
- The brain makes up ~2% of the body's weight but consumes ~20% of the body's total energy
- Brain is sculpted by a lifetime of experiences especially in the first few years of life and during adolescence

- In adult mammals
	- Forebrain grows and surrounds the entire midbrain and part of the hindbrain

- Forebrain
	- Regulate the body's physiology
	- responsible for though and sense perception
- Midbrain

## The Brainstem

- Hindbrain, midbrain, and other central brain structures make the brain stem

## The Limbic System

- Complex set of structures located deep within the brain
	- Involved in emption, memory, and behaviour regulation
- Cingulate gyrus
	- Cortex on lateral walls of groove separating hemisphere (above corpus callosum)
	- Receives and relays information across the brain also involved in emotion regulation, pain perception, and decision making
- Hippocampus
	- Forebrain structure in temporal lobe
	- Formation of new memories and spatial navigation
	- Consolidation information from short-term to long-term memory
- Amygdala
	- Structure in interior temporal lobe
	- Processing emotions
	- Fear, aggression, and emotional memory
- Thalamus
	- Projection fibers connect to the cortical surface to relay sensations, spatial and motor signal information
	- Gateway to higher cortical function
	- Regulates consciousness, sleep, alertness
- Hypothalamus
	- Controls the autonomic nervous system and endocrine system
	- Influences stress responses and emotional reactions
	- Regulates autonomic function
		- Hunger, thirst, temperature control
- Anterior pituitary (master gland)
	- Releases tropic hormones that control secretion/production of hormones in other glans of the endocrine system
	- Not technically part of the limbic system
- Mammillary bodies
	- A protrusion of the bottom of the brain at the posterior end of the hypothalamus
		- Contains some hypothatmic nuclei
		- Serves as relay stations in relfexes related to the sense of smell
- Olfactory bulb
	- A pair of nerve cell clusters in the brain that process information about smells

- Structures that form the epicentre of emotion and behavioural expression
	- Emotions
	- Anxiety
	- PTSD
	- Depression
	- Alzheimer's Disease

# Research Methods

- The main categories of research methods to study the brain include those that attempt to
	- Non invasive
		- Correlate brain anatomy with behaviour
		- Record brain activity during behaviour
	- Invasive
		- Examine the effects of brain damage
		- Examine the effects of stimulating a brain area
## Phrenology

- The process of relating skull anatomy to behaviour
	- One of the first ways used to study the brain
## CT Scan

- Computerized tomography (CT scan)
	- Inject dye into the blood and pass X-rays through the head
	- A person's head is placed into the device and then a rotating sources sends X-rays through the head while detectors on the opposite side make photographs
	- Computer constructs an image of the brain
## MRI Scan

- Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)
	- Applies a powerful magnetic field to image the brain
- MRIs employ powerful magnets which produce a strong magnetic field that forces protons in the body to align with that field
- When a radio frequency current is pulsed the protons are simulated, and spin out of equilibrium, straining against the pull of the magnetic field
- Essentially takes a picture of the brain
	- Primarily grey matter
- People take measurements
	- Cortical thickness
	- Cortical surface area
- Highly sensitive to motion
## fMRI (Functional magnetic resonance imaging)

- Uses the structure from the MRI but then measures function
- Scanner detects the amount of oxygen in the blood in particular parts of the brain during a mental activity
- Great spatial resolution
- Bade temporal because the hemodynamic response takes ~7 seconds
- Highly sensitive to motion
## EEG (Electroencephalography)

- Participants get a head cap with embedded electrodes that record electrical activity from the surface of the scalp
- Good temporal resolution
- Bad spatial resolution
- Best for cortical regions
## PET (Positron Emission Tomography)

- Person is injected with a radio active substance, that circulates in the blood
- Lies in apparatus with radiation detecting scanner
- Measures changes in blood flow areas in the brain in response to specific stimuli
- Timing is much less precise than fMRI
- Expensive
- Most people use fMRA rather than PET
## MEG (Magnetoencephalography)

- Head is enclosed in a scanner that records the magnetic fields produced by electrical activity in the brain
- Enables analysis of activity in the cerebral cortex
- Offers more precision than EEG in terms of spatial location

## NIRS (Near-InfraRed Spectroscopy)

- Thin, flexible optical fibres are attached to a person's scalp and invisible infrared light is beamed at the outer surface of the brain
- As brain responds to stimuli, blood flow and oxygen metabolism are measured
- fNIRS and EEG are good for looking at cerebral cortex, whereas fMRI can be used to look at deep structures in the brain

# Brain damange

- Brain damage can produce an inability to recognize faces, perceive motion, changes in emotional response
	- Ablation
		- Removal of the brain area
	- Lesion
		- Damage to a brain area
			- Stereotaxis instrument
				- Used precisely identify brain structures
	- Injecting a chemical that kills neurons or inactivate then temporarily

## Stimulating a brain area

- Transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS)
	- Application of an intense magnetic field to a portion of the scalp to temporarily deactivate neurons below the magnet
		- Neurostimulator
- Transcranial direct current stimulation (tDCS)
	- Passes small electric current (1-2 mA) through the scalp, skull, and meninges to stimulate the brain
		- Neuromodulator
