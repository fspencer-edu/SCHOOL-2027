- Relatively new scientific discipline

## History of Biopsychology

- Early philosphers
	- Aristotle (~350 BCE)
		- One's mind resided in the heart
	- Hippocrates and Plato (4th centureyBCE)
	- Galen
		- Suggested that since the brain is physically connected to organs and muscles that it is the physical connection to the mind
	- Rene Descartes (1596-1650)
		- French philospher
			- Mind body problem
			- Why certain types of brain activity are conscious
			- Basis of his argument for dualism
			- Described simple reflexes (withdrawal of limb from heat)

- Monism
	- Mind and body are manifestations of a single substance
- Dualism
	- Mind and body are distinct and non identical entities
- Modern neuroscientists support nomism
	- Mental activity and certain types of brain activity are inseparable

- Localization of function
	- Different brain parts and their presumed role in behaviour
- Jean Cesar Legallois (1770-1840)
	- Discovered that when he destroyed tissue in the medulla an animal stopped breaking
- Franz Joesph Gall and Johann Spurzheim
	- Formulated phrenology
		- Measurement of the skull was used to determine personality characteristics of an individual

- Phineas Gage
- Treated by John Martyn Harlow

- Paul Broca 
	- Presented a case where a man has sustained damage to the left frontal lobe and was unable to produce speech
- The Neuron
	- Invention of the microsope and the development of a staining procedure by Camillo Golgi (1873)
- Golgi stain used by Santiago Ramon y Cajal to outline the neural connections in the brain

- Synapse
	- Charles Scott Sherrington (1857-1952)
		- Discover the connection points between neurons
	- Otto Loewi
		- Investigated chemical communication between neurons
			- Neurotransmitters
- Invention of machines
	- Electroencephalograph (EEG) - 1924
	- Single Unit Recording - 1928
	- Positron Emission Tomography (PET) - 1961
	- CT scan - 1971
	- Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) - 1977
	- Functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging - 1990

## What is Biological Psychology?

- A fiend of study that related behaviour to the physical body, especially the brain
- Primary focus on the central nervous system
- Main goal of field is to understand behaviour and experience in terms of biological substrates

1) Perception occurs in the brain
2) Mental activity and certain types of brain activity are inseparable
3) Differences between people can be traced to brain differences

### 1 - Perception occurs in your brain

- Electrical stimulation
- Other senses
- Perception
	- Light
	- Sound

### 2 - Mind and brain are inseparable

- Mental activity and certain types of brain activity are inseparable

### 3 - individual differences

- Our brains are different
	- Physically
	- Functionally
- Behavioural differences are rooted in brain differences

## Biological Explanation of behaviour

- Physiological
	- Related a behaviour to the activity of the brain and other organs
- Ontogenetic
	- Describes how something develops
- Evolutionary
	- Reconstructs the evolutionary history of a structure or behaviour
- Functional
	- Describes why a structure of behaviour evolved as it did
