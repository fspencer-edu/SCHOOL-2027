- Growth and development of neurons
	- 6 kinds of changes
		- Neurogenesis (Proliferation)
		- Migration
		- Differentiation
		- Synaptogenesis
		- Synaptic pruning
		- Myelination

1) Neurogenesis
	1) Proliferation of neurons through cell division
	2) Begins around the 3rd or 4th week of prenatal life
	3) 250,000 new neuron cells are created every minute during 5-25 weeks of age
	4) By week 18, children have almost as many neurons as adults

2) Migration
	1) Movement of new neurons to locations in the nervous system where they ill serve their ultimate functions
		1) Nerve cells
		2) Skin cells
		3) Muscle cells
	2) Occurs in a variety of direction throughout the brain
	3) Immunoglobulins and chemokines guide neuron migration

3) Differentiation
	1) Cell differentiates into a neuron
		1) Forms dendrites, axon, synapses

4) Synaptogenesis
	1) Process where neurons form synapses with each other
	2) Single neuron can form multiple synapses

5) Synaptic pruning
	1) Process where synapses are pruned or eliminated
	2) 40% of superfluous connections are pruned
		1) Apoptosis

6) Myelination
	1) Formation of insulating myeline sheath around the axon that allows signals to travel down axons more quickly

- New neurons later in life
	- Stem cells
		- Undifferentiated cells found in the interior of the brain that generate daughter cell that can transform into glia or neurons

	- Changes in brain wiring occur in response to an individual's unique personal experiences and life circumstances
