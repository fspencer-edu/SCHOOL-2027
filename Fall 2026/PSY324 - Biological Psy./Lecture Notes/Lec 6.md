- Evolutionary interpretations of mating behaviour

- Natural selection
	- Individuals whose genes help them survive will produce more children, and the next generation will have more of these genes
- Sexual selection
	- Genes that make an individual more appealing to the opposite sex will increase the probability of reproduction

- Intrasexual selection
	- Competition between members of the same sex
	- Male fighting for mate
- Intersexual selection
	- Members of one sex choose members of opposite sex

- Interest in multiple mates
	- Two methods for success in spreading a male's genes
		- Loyal to one female and devoting energies toward helping in child rearing
		- Mate with many females and hope some can raise offspring without help

- Humans have 46 chromosomes and 23 pairs
- Mitosis
	- Sister chromatids align at the center of the cell (metaphase) and separate during anaphase into individual daughter chromosomes
- Meiosis
	- A specialized type of cell division that reduces the chromosomes number by half
	- Turns on diploid parent cell into 4 genetically distinct haploid gametes

- Genes in sexual development
	- Genes on the X and Y chromosomes also produce sex differences
	- At least 3 genes on the Y chromosome are active in specific brain areas
	- At least 1 gene on the X chromosome is active only in the female brain

- Organizing effects
	- Sex hormones during prenatal development determines structure of sex organs and brain
	- Postnatal development at puberty determines other sexually dimorphic features
	- Long-term effects
- Activating effects
	- Sex hormones activated during postnatal stage
		- Sperm production
		- Ovulation
		- Sex drive
	- Short term effect
		- Cyclical (females)

	- 0 to 6 weeks
		- Sex organs are undifferentiated
		- Testes/ovaries
	- Mullerian ducts
		- Embryonic precursors of the female internal sex organs
		- Fimbriae, fallopian tubes, uterus, inner 2/3 of vagina
	- Wolffian ducts
		- Embryonic precursors of the male internal sex organs
		- Seminal vesicles, epididymis, vas deferens

- XX or XY (dichotomy)
- Aneuploidy
	- The condition of having an abnormal number of chromosomes in a haploid set

- Turner Syndrome (X0)
- 1/2500 femals
- Either entire X, or partial X chromosome is missue
- Result of defective sperm
- Gonads do not develop
- Internal and external organs are female
- No estrogen, puberty must be induced
- Short stature, broad chested, webbed neck
- Learning disability, increased risk for CVD, immune disorders

- Klinefelter Syndrome (XXY)
	- 1/500 males
	- Additional X from mother's egg of father's sperm
	- Taller than average
	- Greater abdominal fat
	- Low muscle tone
	- Less facial/body hair
	- Small sex organs
	- Infertile

- XX Male Syndrome
	- XX sex reversal
	- 1/25,000 males
	- Genetic sex is female
	- SRY is translocated to X chromosome
	- Male gonads are formed
	- Less hair

- Androgen Insensitivity Syndrome (AIS)
	- 1/99,000 male
	- Genetic sex is male
	- Receptors do not respond to androgens
	- Complete AIS
	- Partial AIS
	- Mild AIS
	- Present with internal testes

- Persistent Mullerian Duct Syndrome
	- Genetic sex is male
	- Extremely rare
	- Failure to produce AMH or no receptors for the hormone
	- Generic variation
	- Autosomal recessive gene
	- Present with female and male internal sex organs

- Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia (CAH)
	- Genetic sex is female
	- 1/15,000 females
	- Autosomal recessie disorders
	- Dysregulation of the adrenal glands (CYP21 gene)
	- Exposure to high androgen levels during development
	- Mullerian system activated, but external organs are intersex/ambiguous

- Organizing effect of sex hormones
	- Female rats exposed to testosterone during sensitive period become partly masculinized in anatomy and behaviour
	- Male rat lacks androgen receptors or is castrated, developed female-like anatomy and behaviour
	- Estradiol and other estrogens do not modify male anatomy
		- Modify various internal structures
			- Prostate gland
	- Absence of sex hormones generally leads to female-looking external genitalia

- Organizing effects of hormones on the brain
	- Sexual dimorphism of hypothalamus largely a result of hormone exposure
	- Female hypothalamus generates cyclic pattern of hormone release
	- Male hypothalamus releases hormones steadily
