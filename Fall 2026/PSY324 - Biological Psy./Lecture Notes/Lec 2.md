# Animal Cells

- Cell member
	- Separates the inside of the cell from the outside environment
- Nucleus
	- Contains the chromosomes/DNA
- Endoplasmic reticulum
	- Network of thin tubes that transports newly synthesized proteins to their location
- Ribosomes
	- Sites at which the cells synthesizes new protein molecules
- Mitochondrion
	- Performs metabolic activities and provides energy that the cell requires

# Neurons - Anatomy

- Neurons
	- Receive information and transmit it to other cells
	- Highly variable shape
- Dendrites
	- Short extensions that pass nerve impulses toward the cell
	- Receives information
- Cell body (soma)
	- Spherical part of the neuron that contains the nucleus, ribosomes, and mitochondria
	- Processes and integrates information
- Nucleus
	- Contains DNA
- Axon
	- Long extensions of the neuron that passes nerve impulses away from the cell body
		- Carries information across long distances from one part of the neuron to another
- Presynaptic terminal
	- Transmits information to another neuron
- Myelin sheath
	- Insulating material
		- Helps with speed of transmission
- Nodes of Ranvier
	- Short specialized regions of axon that are not insulated by myeline
	- Propagate electrical signal

## Neurons

- A motor neuron
	- Has its soma in the spinal cord
	- Receives excitation from other neurons
	- Conducts impulses along its axons to a muscle or gland
- A sensory neuron
	- Is specialized on one end to be highly sensitive to a particular type of simulation
		- touch, light, sound

# Structure and Function of Cells

## Gila

- Microgila
	- Remove waste material, viruses, and fungi from the brain
	- Also remove dead, dying, or damaged neurons
- Oligodendrocytes and Schwann cells
	- Build myelin sheath the surrounds and insulates certain vertebrate axons
	- Oligodendrocytes
		- Brain and spinal cord
	- Schwann cells
		- Periphery of the body
- Radial gila
	- Neural Progenitor cells
	- Help neurons divide and given rise to different types of brain cells
	- Essential for generation of new neurons during brain development
	- Guide the migration of neurons and the growth of their axons and dendrites during embryonic development

# The Blood brain barrier

<img src="/images/Pasted image 20261006195935.png" alt="image" width="500">

- A mechanism that surrounds the brain and blocks most chemicals from entering
- The immune system destroyed damaged or infected cells throughout the body
- Neurons in the brain generally do not regenerate
- Blood brain barrier blocks incoming viruses, bacteria, or other harmful material from entering
- Outside the brain, endothelial cells (cells that form walls of capillaries) are separated by small gaps
- In the brain, endothelial cells join tightly that they block almost anything from passage

- Virus crosses the blood brain barrier
	- Brain's microgila attack
	- Mounts an inflammatory response without killing cell
	- Response controls the virus without eliminating it

# Neural Signals

- Dendrites
	- Receive signals
	- Based on signal strength neuron decides whether or not to pass information along
- Action potential
	- Electrical change that runs down the axon from the axon hillock to the terminal buttons
- If axons used electrical conduction the strength of the signal would decay as it travelled
- Axons periodically regenerate an impulse
- Transmission of signal is dependent on the movement of ions
	- Ions = charge particles
	- Sodium, potassium, and chloride
- Ions are unequally distributed between the inside and outisde of the cell
- Presence and movement of ions is important for when a neuron fires and when the neuron is at rest

## Resting Potential

- Resting potential
	- The difference in electrical charge between the inside and the outside of a neuron when the cell is in a non-excited state
	- Connecting electrodes to a voltmeter
	- Magnitude varies, but a typical level is -70 mV

- When at rest, the membrane maintains
	- Electrical gradient
		- Difference in electrical charge between the inside and outside of the cell
		- Membrane potential
	- Concentration gradient
		- The difference in distribution of ions across the membrane

<img src="/images/Pasted image 20261006200940.png" alt="image" width="500">

- All voltage-gated Na+ channels and most K+ channels are closed
- Sodium-Potassium pump moves 2 K+ into cells and 3 Na+ out of cell

- Electrical gradient + concentration gradient pull sodium ions into the cell
- Helps to maintain electrical gradient
- Body invests energy to operate the sodium-potassium pump that maintains resting potential
- Resting potential prepares the neuron to respond rapidly
- Resting potential remains stable until the neuron is stimulated
- Excitation of neuron opens sodium channels letting sodium enter cell rapidly

	1) At peak action potential, Na+ channels closed while K+ channels open

## Propagation of the Action Potential Facilitated by Myelin

- Myelin sheath
	- Insulating layer that allows electrical impulses to transmit quickly and efficiently along nerve cells
- Nodes of Ranvier
	- Gaps in myelin on facilitates the rapid conduction of nerve impulses
- Saltatory conduction
	- Jumping of action potential from node to node

## Saltatory conduction

<img src="/images/Pasted image 20261006202102.png" alt="image" width="500">

- Fast process where electrical nerve signals jump from one gap to the next along a myelinated nerve fibre
- Signal travels much faster than in unmyelinated fibers
- Ion exchange happens only at the tiny nodes

## Activity within the Neuron

- Principles for conduction of the Action potential
	- All of none law
		- Once an action potential is triggered in an axon, it is propagated without decrement to the end of the fibre
	- Rate law
		- Variations in the intensity of a stimulus or other information being transmitted along an axons are represented by variations in the rate at which that axon fibres (number of APs)
