# Violations of physical and psychological expectations in the human adult brain

## Abstract

- Violation of expectation (VOE) experiments
	- Infants took longer at the same outcomes
- Functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI)
	- Designed to test for physical and psychological expectations in infants
- Domain general processes
	- Visual prediction error and curiosity
- Domain specific processes
	- Prediction error with respect to distinctively physical and psychological expectations
- In adult brains, violations for physical and psychological expectations involved domain, specific, and domain general
- Early visual areas did not respond more strongly because an event was unexpected
- Multiple demand regions in the brain responded more strongly to unexpected events in both the physical and psychological conditions
	- Goal directed attention
	- Problem solving
	- Cognitive effect
- Left supra marginal gyrus (LSMG)
	- Involved when people made predications about physical events and responded strong when a physical expectation was violated

- Measure how long infants look at something
	- Longer than expected means that some expectation was violated

## 1. Intro.

- During the first year of life
	- Infants start forming expectations about how objects and agents behave
- Objects -> Gravity, solidity, and continuous motion
- Agents -> Goal directed and efficient behaviour

- Violation of expectation response (looking time is longer than expected)
- Do not all agree what longer looking means
	- Domain specific
		- Infants have some understanding of domains
	- Stimulus driven
		- Infants might look longer because something in the unexpected event is more visually unusual, novel, or attention grabbing
	- Surprise vs. exploration
		- Reflects detection of a prediction error
		- Curiosity and an attempt to learn more

- Physical/psychological = what kind of expectation is being tested (object/agent)
- Domain-general/domain specific = broad or narrow the brain system processes

- Multiple demand network
	- Objects and agents
	- Domain general
- LSMG
	- Domain specific physical prediction process
### 1.1 Domain-specific hypotheses

- Infants may be born with several separate basic systems of knowledge
	- Physical objects
		- Objects continue to exist when hidden
	- Agents
		- Agents have goals and act in consistent and efficient ways to achieve these goals
- Core knowledge
	- Physics
	- Psychology
	- Number
	- Space
- Infants have some expectations that apply to both agents and objects
	- Solid entities

### 1.2 Domain general hypotheses

- Same broad process could operate whether the event is physical or psychological
- Stimulus driven predication error
	- Different, novel, or unusual
- Curiosity and information seeking
	- Motivate to investigate the learn
- Solidity
	- Bang the object
- Gravity
	- Drop it
- When researchers give infants an explanation for the surprising event, the infants stop exploring the object as much

- Domain specific
	- The infant reacts because a particular rule was violated
- Domain general
	- Reaction could instead come from a broader system

### 1.3 The contribution of functional neuroimaging for testing these hypotheses

- fMRI can help distinguish between the competing explanations for VOE
- Brain imaging can reveal which internal systems are actually responding
	- Psychological events
		- STS
		- TPK
		- MPFC
		- Precuneus
	- Physical events
		- Supamarginal gyrus (SMG)
		- Supplementary motor area
		- Superior parietal cortex

- If VOE is caused by low level visual novelty, early visual areas should respond
	- V1
	- MT
	- Process basic visual features
		- Motion, orientation, speed, and spatial patterns
- Visual response should occur for unexpected events in both physical and psychological conditions
- If VOE reflects curiosity or domain general attention, then the multiple demand MD network should become more active for unexpected events regardless of the domain
	- Frontal and parietal regions
	- Insula
	- Anterior cingulate cortex
	- Demanding tasks, attention, learning, and processing prediction errors

|Hypothesis|Brain regions predicted|Expected pattern|
|---|---|---|
|**Domain-specific physical prediction**|SMG, parietal/motor regions|More activity for unexpected physical events|
|**Domain-specific psychological prediction**|STS, TPJ, MPFC, precuneus|More activity for unexpected psychological events|
|**Visual novelty**|V1, MT|More activity for unexpected events in both domains|
|**Curiosity / general attention**|Multiple-demand network|More activity for unexpected events in both domains|

### 1.4 Overview of current research

- Scanned adults, while they watched the same kinds of physical and psychological events originally created for infant VOE studies
- Subject-specific functional regions of interest (ssfROIs)
	- Used separate, pervasively validates tasks called localizers to identify the relevant functional regions in each individual participant
	- Interpretation of the fMRA activity stronger
- Domain
	- Physical vs. psychological
- Event type
	- Expected vs. unexpected
- Adults is both a strength and a limitation
	- Brain networks are easier to identify using localizer tasks
	- Reduces reverse inference
		- Seeing activity int he brain region and forming a conclusion
	- Limitation is that not identical to infant brain
## 2. Methods

**Experiment 1**

- Pre-register a plan to analyze brain activity across the whole brain
- Publicly specified their hypotheses and analysis plan before looking at the final results
- Reduce the risk of choosing analyses afterward based on all findings
- Did not find a consistent VOE
- Exploratory ROI analyses
	- Looked specifically at brain regions involved in physical reasoning, psychological reasoning, visual processing, or attention

**Experiment 2**

- Large sample, more stimuli, and additional localizer tasks for identifying the relevant brain regions

- Two experiments were similar
### 2.1 Open science practices


- Open science and pre-registration
- Registered their planned methods and analyses before collecting the data
- Stimuli, analysis code, and data publicly available so other researchers can check or reproduce the results
- Shared de-identified brain images for consenting participants

- Some people where excluded because of technical problems or because their fMRA data data had too much motion to be usable
- Normal or corrected vision and could safely undergo MRI scanning

**Experiment 1**

- 20 -> 17 people

**Experiment 2**

- 33 -> 32 people
- Power analysis based on Experiment 1
	- Helps estimate how many participants they need to have a reasonable change of determining a real effect

- 2 tasks
	- Localizer tasks
		- Identify, in each person's brain, the regions associated with things like physical reasoning, social/psychological reasoning, visual processing, and multi-demand attention
		- Used these ROIs when analyzing the main VOE task
	- VOE
		- Measured how that region responds to expected vs. unexpected events

### 2.2 Participants

- Total was 49 participants
### 2.3 Experimental tasks

- Localizer tasks
	- Used to determine where particular cognitive systems where located in each participant's brain
- VOE task
	- Test how that brain region responds to expected vs. unexpected events

#### 2.3.1 Localizer task, Experiment 1

- DOTSIoc
- Watched videos containing two moving dots
- Behaved like physical objects, and other behaved like social agents interacting with each other
- After disappearing, participants predicted where it should reappear

STS -> psychological prediction
SMG -> physical predication

#### 2.3.2 Primary VOE task, Experiment 1

- Goal violation
	- An agent unexpectedly changes its goal
- Efficiency violation
	- An agent takes an inefficient route
- Solidity violation
	- An object appears to pass through another solid object
- Support violation
	- An object behaves impossibly with respect to physical support

- Familiarization video
	- Expected outcome
	- Unexpected outcome
- Fixation task check that they were paying attention
	- Measure brain's natural response to expectation violation, rather than asking people to identify them

#### 2.3.3 Localizer taks, Experiment 2

- DOTSloc
- Same as task used in Experiment 1

- spWMloc
	- Participants saw an 8x8 grid and had to remember the locations of squares the changed colour
- Easy and hard trials were used to identify multiple-demand network
	- Effortful cognition
	- Goal-direction attention
	- Problem solving
	- Working memory
- Domain-general attention/curiosity system
- V1 (Primary visual cortex)

- motionLoc (motion localizer)
	- Watched coherent or randomly moving dots
	- Identify area MT (visual motion)
	- Visual novelty/prediction-error hypothesis

STS -> psychology
SMG -> physics
MD network -> attention/curiosity
VT + MT -> visual processing
#### 2.3.4 Primary VOE task, Experiment 2

- Used more scenarios, including both procedurally generated videos and some hand-animated videos

- Physical events
	- Solidity violtion
	- Permanence violation
- Psychology action events
	- Changing goals
	- Acting inefficiently
- Psychology environment events
	- Agent passes through a solid wall
	- Agent moves around an obstacle that does not exist

- Familiarization movie
- Test movie
- Fixation
- Attention check

- Participants saw either an expected or unexpected test movie on a given trial rather than both
- Attention check tested whether participants remembered something from the video they had just watched
- Randomized stimulus order so that results would not depend on a sequence of videos
### 2.4 Data acquisition

- Structural MRI
	- Produced a detailed anatomical picture of the brain
- Functional MRI
	- Measure BOLD (Blood Oxygen Level Dependent) activity
	- fMRI detects these changes as an indirect measure of neural activity
### 2.5 Neuroimaging data pre-processing and analysis

- Raw fMRI data contain a lot of noise
- fMRIPrep is used to clean and prepare data
- Skull stripping
	- Remove non-brain tissue from MRI image
- Segmentation
	- Separate the brain into
		- Gray metter
		- White matter
		- Cerebrospinal fluid
- Registration
	- Align each person's brain to a common reference brain called MNI152
- Motion correction
	- Estimate movement and identified frames with excessive motion
		- FD - framewise displacement
		- DVARS - changes in signal between adjacent scans
	- Head motion can look like brain activity
- GLM analysis
	- General linear models to estimate how much brain activity was associated with each experimental condition
		- Unexpected physical
		- Expected physical
		- Unexpected psychological
		- Expected psychological

### 2.6 Localizing functional ROIs in individual participants

- Subject specific functional ROI (ssfROI)

	- Physical prediction
	- Visual processing
	- Goal directed attention

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Results in focal regions

#### 3.1.1 Physics ROIs - SMG

- Both left and right SMG preferred physical over psychological events
- These regions are involved in physical processing
- Left SMG (LSMG) showed a domain x event interaction
- Did not react when something surprising happened, only when a physical expectation was violated
- RSMG did not show the same results

LSMG = strongest evidence for a specialized physical VOE mechanism

#### 3.1.2 Psychology ROIs - STS

- Both left and right STS responded more to psychological than physical events
- VOE finding was weaker
- Did not find consistent evidence that it specifically computes psychological prediction errors
- No consistent domain specific psychological VOE effect in STS

#### 3.1.3 Early visual Physics ROIs - V1 and MT

- Visual novelty hypothesis
- If VOE were caused by unexpected events, early visual regions should show
- Neither V1 or MT distinguished unexpected from expected
- Did respond to visual novelty in another analysis
- Early visual processing does not explain the VOE effect
#### 3.1.4 Goal directed attention ROIs - RFC and APC

- Domain general VOE process
- RFC (right frontal cortex) and APC (anterior parietal cortex)
- These regions cared about unexpectedness, rather than whether the violated expectation was physical or psychological
- Supports the hypothesis that VOE involves domain general endogenous or goal directed attention
- MD network = domain general response to surprise
### 3.2 Controlling for visual statistics

- Statistically differences in
	- Motion
	- Luminance
	- Constrast
	- Spatial frequency
	- Straightness
	- Curvature
- LSMG physical specific VOE effect remained
- RFC/APC unexpected > expected effects remained
- STS and V1/MT null VOE results remained
### 3.3 Exploring domain and event affected in additional ROIs

- None of the additional ROIs showed a significant VOE effect after the stricter statistical correct
- Many regions showed strong preference for physics vs. psychology

- Domain specific networks appear organized by what kind of information was being processed, rather than what is surprising

- Domain general were not dedicated exclusively to one domain
	- Stable domain preferences
	- Some table event/VOE preferences across domains

### 3.3 Multivariate tests of event and domain information

- No reliable spatial activity pattern distinguishing unexpected from expected events in the focal regions
- Found strong spatial patterns
	- Psychology vs physical
- Prediction error may increase overall neural response magnitude without creating a high consistent voxel-by-voxel representation of surprise
### 3.3 Neural responses to physically surprising outcomes, revealed by an agent's action

- Right STS showed a significant VOE response
	- Agent and physical reasoning
- When a agent's action reveals something physically impossible, the brain recruits
	- Physical processing + social processing + domain general attention

## 4. Discussion

- Did not strongly find early visual novelty mechanisms
- Did find domain general goal direction attention
- Found domain specific physical process for physical violations
- VOE involves multiple cognitive systems
### 4.1 Violations of physical expectations

- LSMG
	- Intuitive physics system
- MD regions also respond
- Physical violation
	- Domain specific physical prediction error
	- Domain general attention
- LSMG -> RFC/APC

### 4.2 Violations of psychological expectations

- Unexpected irrational actions also activate domain general attention system
- RFC/APC becomes more active
- Psychological violations may have been to easy to detect STS

### 4.3 Distinct and shared representations across intuitive physics and psychology

- Physics and psychology are partly separate, and partly integrated systems
- Many brain regions prefer
	- Agents or physical objects

### 4.4 Further limitations

- Two domains
	- Intuitive physics
	- Intuitive psychology
- Does not generalize to
	- Numerical expectation
	- Social relationships
	- Learned statistical patterns
	- Other types of knowledge
- Selected set of cortical systems
- Performed on adults, not infants

### 4.5 Conclusion

- Unexpected physical event
	- LSMG -> physical specific prediction error
	- MD regions -> increased general attention
- Unexpected psychological event
	- MD regions -> strong general attention response
	- STS -> not consistent specific prediction error response
- V1/MT did not show a VOE response
