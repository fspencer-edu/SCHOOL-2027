# Physiological Factors and Barriers Affecting Resistance

- Factors include but are not limited to
	- Age
	- Nutrition
	- Hormones
	- Fever
	- Genetic factors
	- Dual infections
	- Species resistance

- Age
	- Newborns and the elderly most vulnerable
	- Newborns
		- Passive immunity from mothers
	- Elderly
		- Become more vulnerable as immune system declines with age
	- Some exceptions
		- 1918 influenze A
		- Herpesvirus
- Nutrition
	- Malnutrition impairs
		- Skin and mucosal barriers
		- Innate and adaptive immunity
	- Vitamin A deficiency (VAD)
		- Mortality increase from infectious disease
		- During famine, mortality may reach 50%
	- Vitamin A
		- Used in supplementation programs
		- Acts as a vaccine adjuvant
			- Substance that enhances immune response
- Hormones
	- Certain hormones therapies increase viral susceptibility
		- Glucocorticoids
		- Stress increases neuroendocrine hormones
	- Astronaut studies
		- Reactivation of latent herpesviruses
		- Elevated stress hormones
- Fever
	- Normal body temperature is 37 celcuis
	- Regulated by the hypothalamus
	- Fever is when elevation above normal
		- Infection
		- Inflammation
		- Trauma
	- Triggered by pyrogens
		- Endogenous (cytokines)
		- Exogenous (microbial products)
	- Cryogens limit excessive temperature rise
	- Balance determines fever intensity and duration
- Genetic Factors
	- Viral immunity has a genetic component
	- ~5% of population resistant to HIV-1
	- CCR5-$\Delta$ 32 mutation
		- Premature stop codon
		- Prevents HIV-1 entry
- Dual infections
	- Opportunistic infections hallmark of HIV
	- Result from CD4+ T helper cells depletion
	- Respiratory viral infections predispose to secondary infections
		- Influenza A virus leads to bacterial pneumonia

# Host Defenses

- Species resistance
	- Host range
		- Cell/species a virus can infect
	- Determined by
		- Virus-receptor compatibility
		- Intracellular host factors
- Immune system
	- Distinguishes self vs non self
	- Eliminates harmful non self antigens
		- Antigen is any molecule recognized by the immune system
	- Physical barriers
		- Skin
		- Cornea
		- Respiratory, GI, GU mucosa
- Innate and Adaptive Immunity
	- Innate immunity
		- Immediate
		- No prior exposure
		- No memory
	- Adaptive immunity
		- Delayed
		- Antigen specific
		- Immunological memory

## Innate Immunity

- Step 1 - Mechanical Immunity
	- Skin
		- Dead cell layer, impermeable
	- Mucous membranes
		- Mucines
		- Tears, mucus, urine
		- Acidic environments (stomach, vagina)
		- Ciliated cells remove trapped viruses
- Step 2 - Phagocytosis
	- Engulf pathogens
	- Macrophages
		- Ingest invaders
		- release cytokines
	- Cytokines
		- Recruit immune cells
		- Trigger inflammation
- Step 3 - Dendric cells
	- Found in tissues exposed to environment
		- Skin
		- Mucosa
		- Respiratory and GI tract
	- Professional antigen-presenting cell (APCs)
	- Process antigen and present via MHC molecules

<img src="/images/Pasted image 20261019230655.png" alt="image" width="500">

- Step 4 - Natural Killer (NK) cells
	- Recognize virally infected cells
	- Kill infected and cancer cells via membrane disruption

- Step 5 - Host cell response
	- Infected cells release cytokines
	- Interferons (IFNs)
		- Antiviral signalling molecules
	- Infected cells present antigen via MHC molecules

# T Cell Mediated Immunity

- B cells recognize whole antigens
- t cells recognize infected cells
- T cell types
	- CD8+ cytotoxic cells ($T^c$)
	- CD4+ helper cells ($T^h$)

- Cytotoxic T cells (CTLs)
	- Recognize infected cells via MHC
	- Release cytotoxic proteins
	- Induce apoptosis
- Immunological Memory
	- ~ 5 x 10^10 lymphocytes produce daily
	- Each has unique receptor
	- First infection
		- 5-10 days to respond
		- Subsequent infections
			- Faster
			- Often asymptomatic

# Viral Evasion Strategies

- Rapid mutation
- Antigen decoys
- Cytokine inhibition
- Immune cell inactivation

- Vaccination
	- Passive immunization
		- Immediate, short term protection
		- No memory
	- Active immunization
		- Delayed
		- Long-term protection
		- Immunological memory

- Passive immunization
	- Transfer of antibodies
	- Natural
		- Maternal to fetus
	- Artificial
		- Antiserum therapy
	- Antibodies last ~2-3 weeks
	- Used for
		- Hepatitis A
		- Ebola
		- Measles
- Active immunization
	- Induced by
		- Natural infection
		- Vaccination
	- Stimulate host immune system

- Vaccines
	- Vaccine
		- Product that contains a weakened or dead pathogenic microbe or parts of microbes that is used to stimulate immune resistance toward a specific pathogen
	- Vaccination
		- The inoculation with a vaccine through an injection, by mouth (orally), or aerosol (nasal) spray in order to immunize an individual

- Types of vaccines
	- Traditional
		- Killed/inactivated vaccines
		- Live attenuated viruses
	- New Generation
		- Recombinant subunits
		- Live vectors
		- DNA vaccines
		- RNA vaccines

- Traditional vaccines
	- Killed or inactivated vaccines
		- Headt, UV, chemicals
			- Salk vaccine against poliovirus
	- Live, attenuated viruses
		- Use of weakened or avirulent mutatnt viruses
		- Carry surface antigens, but cannot penetrate the cells
			- Sabin vaccine against poliovirus

		- RNA vaccines
