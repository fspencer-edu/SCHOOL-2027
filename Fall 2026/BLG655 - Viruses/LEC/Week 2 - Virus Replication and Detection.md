# Basic Steps of Viral Replication

<img src="/images/Pasted image 20261019114450.png" alt="image" width="500">

- Key Steps of the Viral replication cycle
	- Attachment (via surface proteins)
	- Penetration (entry of virus into a cell)
	- Uncoating (disassembly and localization)
	- Genome replication and gene expression
	- Assembly of progeny viruses
	- Egress/Release

- Step 1 - Attachment
	- Viruses exhibit tropism for a particular cell type
	- Host range
		- Different types of organisms that the virus can infect
			- Broad range (rabies)
			- Narrow range (HIV)
	- Viral attachment proteins bind cell surface receptors on the cell membrane
	- Some viruses require engagement of several membrane components (coreceptors)

- Step 2 - Penetration
	- Naked or enveloped viruses enter

- Step 3 - Uncoating (Disassembly and Localization)
	- Removal or degradation of capsid and releasing the genome
	- Localization of the genome to the nucleus

- Step 4 - Genome Replication and Gene Expression
	- Two events critical to viral infection
		- Production of virus structural proteins and enzymes
		- Replication of the virus genome (dsDNA, ssDNA, dsRNA, ssRNA)

- Step 5 - Assembly
	- Assembly begins when the appropriate concentration of virus proteins and viral genomes is reached
	- Viral factories
		- Specific cellular compartments in the infected cell
		- Locations of viral factories differ according to the virus
		- Cellular and viral components are used to form viral factories
		- Viruses to hide from host cell anti-viral defenses

- Viral factories
	- Mimivirus
	- African swine fever virus

- Step 6 - Egress/release
	- Lytic viruses
		- Egress the host cell upon killing of the cells
	- Budding viruses
		- Egress through the plasma membrane
		- Cell remains alive

- Any of the viral replication steps could be targeted for aniviral intervention

- 5 approaches for lab diagnosis of viral infections
	- Detection of virions
	- Detection of virus antigens (surface proteins)
	- Detection of infectivity
	- Detection of virus genomes
	- Detection of host antibodies

## Detection of virions

- Electron microscopy
	- Used to observe the morphology of individual virus particles
	- Rapid and useful if viruses cannot be replicated in cell cultures
	- Expensive, limitations
	- Need million of virions/ml

## Viral Antigen Detection

- Enzyme linked immunosorbert assay (ELISA)
	- Inexpensive
	- Easy to perform
	- Rapid turnaround
	- Can be used to detect viral antigens or viral antibodies our immune system makes again viral antigens
- Clinical sample is added to wells precoated with virus-specific antibodies
- If viral antigens are present in the clinical sample, they will bind to the virus-specific antibodies
- Colour will develop if antigen binds to antibody
- Test is positive

## Detection of Infectivity

- Cell cultures used for virus isolation and identification
	- Cell culture are cells isolated from human tissues and grown on plastic
	- Diagnostic laboratories use multiple cell types isolated from different tissues
	- Clinical specimen is used to infect cell cultures susceptible to the infection
	- Monitor for cytopathic effects (CPE)
		- Changes in cell appearance indicative of infection

-  Cytopathic Effects (CPEs)
	- Visual changes in cells from viral infections
		- Rounding of the cells
		- Increased retractility
		- Cell lysis/death

- 4 - Genome Detection
	- Detect viral genes
		- PCR (DNA viruses)
		- PT-PCR (RNA viruses)
	- Diagnosis
	- Management of patients
		- GIV, hepatitis C patients
		- Monitor viral load
	- Detect DNA sequence of the entire virus
		- Next generation genome sequencing
		- Monitoring new strains/variants

- Polymerase chain reaction (PCR)
	- Molecular biology technique
	- Amplified specific DNA sequences
	- Used to detect the presence of a virus
	- Amplifies specific viral genes, often those encoding surface proteins
	- Indicate infection if viral DNA is detected
	- Valuable for early detection when pathogen numbers are low
	- Widely used in diagnostics, forensics, and genetic research
- reverse transcription RT PCR
	- used to amplify RNA viruses
		- RNS is converted to DNA, then PCR is performed

- Step 5 - Antibody Detection

- Useful in identifying viruses that are difficult to grow in cell culture or that cause diseases with slow courses
- Presence of antibodies are an indirect measure of viral infection
	- Patient serum contains antibodies
		- IgM antibodies show up first
			- Indicate acute infection
		- IgG antibodies show up later in the infection
			- Can also indicate chronic infection

- In vitro experiments
	- Laboratory trials that use isolated viruses, isolated cells, components of viruses such as DNA/RNA/proteins
	- Study viral replciation
	- Solve structure of viral proteins
	- Study effects of antivirals on viruses grown in cells
	- Develop vaccines

- Advantages
	- Species-specific, simpler, more convenient, and more detailed analysis than can be down with the whole organism
	- Automated, miniaturized, high throughput
- Limitations
	- Experiments do not recapitulate the complexity of human body
	- Antivirals need to be etst on a whole organsim

- Animal or in viva experiments
	- Test with animal models
	- Starting point for future research on humans
		- Antiviral or vaccine testing before applied to humans
	- Advantages
		- Feasible
		- Whole body system test
		- Ethical alternative to human testing
		- Test subject for their entire life span
	- Limitations
		- Human physiology is different from animals
		- Antivirals can act different
