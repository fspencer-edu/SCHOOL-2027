- What is a cell
	- Cells are the basic building blocks of all living things
	- Every structure in the body is made of cells
	- Cells are complex and not visible by the naked eye
	- Cells can be visualized using light microscopes (1000x)

- Cell have many shapes and sizes, depending on the jobs they do
- Cells become specialized
	- Different function and structure
		- Epithelial cell
		- Fat cell
		- Nerve cell
		- Olfactory neuron
		- Red blood cell
			- Most abundant cell type in the human body
			- >80%
		- Retinal rod
- There are over 30 T cells in a single person
- >200 types of cells

- Building materials
	- Water
	- Proteins
	- Fats
	- Carbohydrates
	- Minerals

- Essential features
	- Plasma membrane
	- Nucleus
	- Cytoplasm
		- ER
		- Golgi complex
	- Mitochondria

- Cells build tissues
	- Cells with similar structure and function are grouped together in tissues
	- 4 types of tissues in the body
		- Epithelium
			- Cover parts of the body and organs
			- Connective tissues
				- Fibroblasts, blood, immune cells
			- Muscle tissue
			- Nervous tissue

- Cell functions
	- Specialized functions
		- Protective layer
		- Transport of oxygen and nutrients
		- Immune respomse
		- Structure
		- Nerve impulse
	- Communicate with each other
	- Move substance across the cell membrane
	- Make new cells through division

- How do cells perform their function
	- All cells must make specialized proteins that are used to execute various functions of cells
		- Enzymatic proteins
		- Storage proteins
		- Hormonal proteins
		- Motor proteins
		- Defensive proteins
		- Transport proteins
		- Receptor proteins
		- Structural proteins

- How are proteins made
	- DNA is a highly stable set of molecule the encodes for all that cells need to known how to build out bodies
	- RNA provides a user manual to make specific proteins
- Proteins
	- Made of amino acids

- The Central Dogma
	- Describes the flow of information in cells
		- DNA codes for ribonucleic acid, or RNA, which codes for proteins
		- RNA copy made of the DNA's information
		- RNA copy is read to determine what building block to use to make a protein

- RNA synthesis
	- Transcription is the process of using a DNA template to make a complementary RNA
- Protein synthesis
	- Translation is the process of using the information in mRNA to synthesize proteins

- DNA (Deoxyribonucleic acid)
	- Double stranded
	- Sugar phosphate backbone
	- Nucleotides
		- Adenine (A)
		- Guanine (G)
		- Cytosine (C)
		- Thymine (T)
- Complementary base pairing
	- A = T
	- C $\equiv$ G

- DNA carries genes
	- A gene is a section of DNA that influences one or more hereditary traits
		- A gene has information to make a specific protein that cell will need to function
		- Not all genes are used by all the cells
		- Cells will only use genes that make proteins required to perform their function

- Genome
	- All the genes in an individual cell comprise its genome
	- Human genome has billion of nucleotides in the DNA of every cell
	- Protein encoding genes in human genome is roughly 25,000
	- Protein encoding genes in the viral genome is about 2-200

- RNA (Ribonucleic acid)
	- Single stranded
		- Sugar backbone backbone
	- Nucleotides
		- Adenine (A)
		- Guanine (G)
		- Cytosine (C)
		- Uracil (U)

- Transcription
	- Genetic information is first written into RNA
	- One strand of DNA duplex is copied into RNA
	- Enzyme RNA polymerase transcribes DNA into mRNA

- Amino acids are protein building blocks
	- Proteins are made up of amino acids
	- The sequence in mRNA is translated into a sequence of amino acids that builds a specific protein
	- Each amino acid contains 3 bases
	- A group of 3 bases that specifies a particular amino acid is called a codon

- Protein shape is flexible
	- Each protein has a characteristic folded shape that is necessary for its function
	- Many proteins have a disordered shape when they are inactive
	- When the active protein is needed, it folds into an ordered, active conformation
	- Some proteins are regulated by controlled when or where they are folded into active shapes
- Protein functions are as diverse as protein structures
	- Catalysis
		- Speed up chemical reactions
	- Defense
		- Antibodies attack pathogens
	- Movement
		- Move cells or molecules within cells
	- Signalling
		- Convey signals between cells
	- Structure
		- Shape cells and comprise body structures
	- Transport
		- Allow muscles to enter and exit cells to carry them throughout the body

- All cells in the body contain the same genetic material
- Differentiation occurs when cells mature and express selective genes to obtain changes in their functions to perform their roles

# Intro to Viruses

- What are viruses
	- Viruses are no cells, but particles
	- 2 main components
		- Protein coat which surrounds and protects their genome
	- Viruses are obligate parasites
		- Cannot do anything on their own
		- Penetrate a living cell
		- Inert until they infect a living cell
		- Hijack cell organelles and use what why need
		- Kill the cell in the proess
		- Obtain essential components from living cells to replicate themselves and complete their life cycle
- Characteristics of viruses
	- Small than bacteria
		- 20-300 nm in length
	- Dependent on host cell to reproduce
		- Use cell's protein machinery to make proteins
	- Genome consists of DNA or RNA
		- DNA viruses
		- RNA viruses
	- Receptor binding protein
		- Viral attachment surface proteins adheres to receptors proteins present on the host cell

- Comparison of viruses and cells
	- Virus
		- Made up of genome protected by a protein coat
		- Contain either DNA or RNA
		- Do not need energy
		- Do not respond to environment
		- Do not grow or develop
		- Do not control internal environment
	- Cell
		- Cell membrane, cytoplasm, genome, and organelles
		- DNA and RNA
		- Needs energy
		- Responds to external and internal environment
		- Grows and develops
		- Maintains a table internal environment

- Tulipomania
	- Broken bulb was unique, less vigorous, making it desirable and expensive
	- A plain virus made certain tulips more valuable

- Germ Theory (1800s)
	- Louis Pasteur and Robert Koch
	- Infectious diseases are caused by microbes
	- Bacteria were first microbes discovered
	- Bacteria was isolated using Chamberland Porcelain Ultrafilters (Charles Chamberland)

- Chamberland Porcelain Ultra Filters
	- Force water through porcelain porous enough to allow passage of the liquids and not tiny particles such as bacteria
	- Bacteria gets stuck in filter
	- Filter water is not infectious
	- Agents that can pass through filters that trap most bacteria
- Tobacco mosaic virus (TMV)

- Visualizing viruses with transmission microscopy
	- Transmission electron microscope (TEM) invented in 1931
	- Uses electrons focused by magnets to resolve find details of structures as small as 0.2 nm
	- Can magnify objects up to 2 million times

- Advances in cultivating viruses (1930s to 1950s)
	- 1930s
		- Developed to grow some viruses in embryonated chicken eggs
	- 1940s
		- First vaccines to prevent influenza viruses
	- 1950s
		- Cultivate large quantities of viruses in cells in the dishes
	- 1955
		- First vaccine to prevent polioviruses

	- Progressive theory
		- Pieces of DNA broke from chromosomes
		- Able to jump from one chromosome or from one cell to another
	- Regressive theory
		- Derived from bacteria that have regressed from a single cell organisms to obligatory parasites
