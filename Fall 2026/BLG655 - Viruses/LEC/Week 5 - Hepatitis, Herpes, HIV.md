# Hepatitis

- History of Hepatitis
	- Inflammation of the liver
		- Classic symptom is jaundice
		- Caused by a number of viruses and sometimes alcohol or prescription drugs
	- Epidemics in history
		- War
			- Crowding and unsanitary conditions of military encampments created an idea environment for hepatitis A viruses to be transmitted
		- WWI
			- Trench warfare

- Hepatitis Virus Nomencalture
	- Each named with a leter
		- Hepatitis A virus (HAV)
		- Hepatitis A virus (HBV)
	- Hepatitis non-A, non-B viruses
		- Hepatitis C (HCV)
		- Hepatitis D (HDV)
		- Hepatitis E (HEV)
		- Hepatitis G (HGV)

- Clinical features of viruses that cause primary hepatitis
	- HAV
		- Most outbreaks associated with contaminated food or water supplied
			- Shellfish may become contaminated with sewage and may concentrate and retain viruses
		- Major mode of transmission
			- Fecal-oral
		- Average incubation period
			- 30 days incubation period
		- Adults experience signs and symptoms more often than children

- HAV
	- Symptoms
		- Fatigue
		- Abdominal pain
		- Loss of appetitie
		- Nausea and vomiting
		- Dark urine
		- Jaundice

- Jaundice
	- Yellow colour in the skin, mucous membranes, or eyes
	- Occurs when liver is not functioning properly
	- Yellow pigment is from bilirubin
		- A byproduct of old red blood cells

- HBV
	- Relatively rare in developed countries
	- Endemic areas
		- Major models of spread is mother (carrier) to infant
	- Other high risk groups
		- IV drug users
		- Hemodialysis patients
		- Individuals with multiple sexual partners
		- Instituitionalized partients
		- Healthcare workers
	- Average incubation period
		- 80 days
	- 30% of individuals have no signs or symptions
		- Similar symptoms to HAV with addition of joint point
	- Chronic HBV
		- 5-10% cases
	- Chronic infections
		- Cirrhosis of liver
		- Hepatocellular (liver) carcinoma cancer (HCC)

- HCV
	- Many infected people show few or no signs of disease for years and event decades
	- HCV infection is common in the developed world
	- Transmission
		- Incubation period
			- 6-7 weeks
		- Spread almost exclusively through blood contact
	- Individuals who received blood products before 1992 are at risk for having contracted HCV
	- If signs or symptoms are present, they are similar to other hepatitis infections
	- Between 55% and 85% of infected persons experience a chronic infections, resulting in live disease


- Laboratory diagnosis of viral hepatitis infections
	- Diagnosis of acute hepatitis is based on
		- Symptoms and physical findings
			- Fatigue, abdominal pain, loss of appetite, darkening of urine
		- Blood test for elevated liver enzymes
		- Elevated antibodies against a virus (serology testing, ELISA)
		- Detection of viral proteins or genomes (nucleic acid tests)
	- Diagnosis of chronic hepatitis is more difficult
		- No symptoms until liver damage is quite advanced
		- Inflammation of liver (cirrhosis)

- Hepatitis virus replication cycles
	- Hepatitis viruses enter the bloodstream
	- Carried to the liver
	- Infect the hepatocytes
	- As hepatocytes damaged, liver cirrhosis occurs and liver function impaired

# Management and prevention of Hepatitis viruses

- HAV
	- Management
		- No specific treatment
		- Supportive care
	- Prevention
		- Handwashing and proper disposal of human feces
	- Vaccines
		- Formalin-inactivated
		- Cell culture-produced
		- Whole virus
- HBV
	- Management
		- Lamivudine
			- Inhibits genome replication
		- Tenovir
			- Reverse transcriptase inhibitor
		- Infereon $\alpha-2b$
			- Cessation of viral replication (effective in only 45% of people)
	- Vaccines
		- Recombinant DNA vaccine in yeast
		- HBV vaccine prevents HBV-related liver cancer

- HCV
	- Management
		- 85% of infectious progress to chronic liver disease
		- Interferon and/or ribavirin
		- Close monitoring of interferon/ribavirin treatment necessary due to adverse reactions
		- 50% of patients cannot tolerate side effects, such as anemia
		- Response of patients depends on viral genotype
	- Pan genotypic direct acting antivirals (DAAs)
		- Successful combination drugs become available proving HCV is a curable disease
			- Works on specific HCV genotypes
			- Very expensive
			- Cost is barrier to treatment
			- Inaccessible to low and middle income countries

# Varicella Zoster Virus (VZV)

- Causes chicken pox and shingles
- Chickenpox
	- Mild disease, afflicts children
	- Can be severe in infants, adults, and immunocompromised individuals
	- Only Herpesvirus that spreads through airborne mode of transmission (coughing or sneezing)

# Chickenpox

- Symptoms develop 10-21 days after contact with an infected person
- VZV infects the skin or respiratory mucosa and progresses through the blood and lymphatic system to the cells involved in phagocytosis
- First sign of disease is the itchy exanthematous rash
- Adult complications
	- Pneumonia
	- Bacterial infection of the skin
	- Encephalitis (swelling of the brain)
- Today children can be vaccinated with the Varivax vaccine at 12-18 months or before their 13th birthday

# Shingles or Herpes Zoster

- After a primary VZV infection (chickenpox), the virus remains latent in the dorsal root ganglia (spinal nerve)
- The virus is most commonly reactivated later in life after the age of 60
- Onset of shingles is more common and severe in immunocompromised patients

- Symptoms of shingles
	- Severe pain, headache, numbness, itching
	- Followed by a rash that forms in 3-5 days
		- Rash follows a nerve on one side of the body
		- In a healthy individual, disease lasts 10-15 days
		- 1/5 severse chronic pain

- Laboratory diagnosis of herpesvirus infections
	- Most definitive test is the presence of viable herpesvirus in a clinical specimen
		- Oral and genital lesions
		- Ocular samples
		- Biopsies
		- Cerebrospinal fluid

# Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV)

- History
	- 1980s
		- Physicians in New York and California made the first observations of a new infectious disease of homosexual men
		- Sudden appearance of rare skin cancer called Kaposi's sarcoma
		- Atypical pneumonic caused by Pneumpcystis carinii
		- CMV infections
		- Oral Candidiasis

- Gay related immune deficiency (GRID)
	- All observed individuals suffered from immune supression
	- 1981
		- The syndrome was named GRID
	- 1982
		- Cases of AIDS in hemophilia patients who received blood transfusions
		- The disease was renamed Acquired Immune Deficiency Syndrome (AIDS)
	- 1983
		- Warning to blood banks

- The discovery of HIV
	- Two research teams
		- Robert Gallo's team
			- National institute for Health (NIH), USA
		- Luc Montagnier's
			- Pasteur Institute, France
	- Both described a retrovirus isolated from cultured $T_H$ lymphocytes derived from a lymp node biopsy of an AIDS patient with lymphadenopathy
	- Montagnier's team was the first to isolated HIV-2
		- Rare in North America
		- Endemic in Western Africa
		- Less infectious and progresses more slowly to AIDS

- HIV/AIDS origin hypothesis (The Hunter Theory)
	- Most commonly accepted hypothesis
	- HIV is a viral zoonosis
		- A virus related to HIV-1, called $SIV_{cpz}$ that infects chimpanzees cross the species barrier
		- Naturally infected chimps were killed for food and eaten
		- Blood entered cuts or wounds on the hunter
- HIV-2 Origin
	- HIV-2 entered the human population as a zoonosis
	- Sooty mangabeys are native to Guinea Bissau, Gabon, and Cameroon, and naturally carry $SIV_{SM}$
	- $SIV_{SM}$ from sooty mangabeys likely evolved into HIV-2 when humans acquired it from hunting sooty mangabeys for food
	- Also, through contaminated blood transfusions and mass vaccination efforts during the independence war in Guinea Bissau
	- 10% of the general population in Guinea Bissau is HIV-2 sero-positive

- HIV Transmission
	- HIV is present in blood, semen, vaginal fluids, breast milk, saliva (low levels), and tears (low levels)
	- Most common ways HIV can be transmitted
		- Anal and vaginal intercourse
		- Sharing of contaminated needles (IV drug users)
		- Blood transfusions (using infected blood or blood products)
		- Accidental needle stick injuries
		- Sharing HIV-contaminated tattoo needles, razors, acupuncture needles, ear piercing implements

- The Fragility of HIV in the Environment
	- Soap and water easily disrupts HIV
	- HIV is highly susceptible to drying out
	- Contact with sweat, saliva, or tears has never been sown to result in transmission
	- Handshaking and hugging has not resulting in transmission
	- Mosquitoes or other insects cannot transmit HIV
- Prevention of HIV Infection
	- Abstinence from sex
	- Use of barrier methods during sexual activity
	- Microbicides
	- No needle sharing
- Global Epidemiology of HIV/AIDs
	- Reasons that HIV patients who are not receiving treatment are left behind
		- Human rights violations, stigma, and discrimination
		- Inequalities in access to treatment and services
		- Gender based inequalities
		- criminalization and exclusion
	- Sub Saharan Africa
		- At least 12 M African children have lost one or both parents to AIDS
		- Access to treatment is improving
	- Central Asia and Eastern Europe
		- HIV incidence increased by more than 25% in 5 countries in Eastern Europe and Central Asia between 2001 and 2009
		- Primary route of transmission is intravenous drug use
	- India and China
		- India has the 3rd largest number of people in the world living with HIV after Nigeria and South Africa
		- China is home to one of the largest injection uses population in the world
	- US and Canada
		- The number of new HIV infections in the US and Canada remains stable
		- The number of HIV positive diagnoses and AIDS related deaths in Canada and USe have both been in decline
		- ART treatment has slowed the progression of HIV/AIDs in many infected individuals

- Clinical symptoms of HIV/AIDS
	- HIV infection destroys CD4+ $T_H$ lymphocytes
	- 3 phases of HIV-1 infection
		- Primary acute HIV-1 infection
		- Chronic asymptomatic phase of infection
		- AIDS
- Acute infection
	- Occurs within 2-4 weeks of HIV infection
	- Interval between the appearance of detectable HIV RNA and the first detection of antibodies
	- Some people experience flu-like symptoms called acute retroviral syndrome (ARS), or primary HIV infection

- Clinical Latency
	- Neither signs nor symptoms of the disease are present
	- Body has fylly developed a positive IgG antibody response
	- Without ART, people living with HIV who are typical progressors advanced to AIDs within 10-12 years


- Acquired Immunodefiiency Syndrome (AIDS)
	- AIDS is defined as a CD4+ $T_H$ lymphocyte cell count below 200 cells$\micro L$ of blood and/or one or more opportunistic infections
	- AIDS patients also suffer from malignancies such as Kaposi's sarcoma and non Hodgkin's lymphoma
	- Wasting syndrome is also common
		- A loss of more than 10% of body weight due to fever or diarrhea for more than 30 days

# HIV Virion Structure

- Retroviridae family
- Lentivirus genus
- Enveloped 100 nm icosahedron shaped sphere
	- gp120 and gp41 spikes
- A nucleocapsid protein, p24, covers the two copies of the (+ssRNA) viral genome
- Reverse transcriptase

- HIV entry
	- Early course of infection
		- HIV infects macrophages
	- Later in the course of infection
		- HIV infects T lymphocytes
	- HIV enters cells via a cellular receptor and coreceptors
		- Major cellular receptor
			- CD4 present on T-lymphocytes (also present in low concentrations on macrophages)
			- Coreceptor on T lymphocytes = CXCR4 (fusion)
			- Coreceptor on macrophages = CCR5

- Managing HIV Patients
	- CD4+ T lymphocyte coutes
	- Virus load monitoring
		- Used to make recommendations for antiviral treatment of patients
		- Measure HIV-1 RNA copies per ml of plasm
			- 100,000 copies per ml of plasma = clinical AIDS
			- An absence of detectable RNA copies suggests a slower progress to AIDS

- HIV Antiviral Therapy
	- Nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors
	- Non-nucleoside reverse transcription inhibitors
	- Protease inhibitors
	- Fusion inhibitors
	- CCR5-blocking entry inhibitors
	- Integrase inhibitors
	- Pharmacokinetic enhancers
- Still no HIV vaccine
- An AIDS patient harbours 100 M genetically distinct variants of HIV
- HIV variants can escape both antibody and T cell-immune responses
