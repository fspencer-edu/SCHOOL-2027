# Virus Transmission

- Viruses are released from infected cells into the environment of the host and infect other susceptible cells
- Viruses must also find new cells to infect
	- Adjacent cells or distant cell (transport via blood)
- Virus infections could modify the host behaviour to ensure transmission
	- Aggression of mammals with rabies
- Viruses must survive harsh conditions and long journeys
	- Rivers and winds
	- Migration
	- Human travel
	- Animal export

# Disease Transmission

- Direct transmission
	- Kissing, Sex, touching
	- Coughing
	- Animal bite
	- Mother-infant
- Indirect transmission
	- Contaminated food/water
	- Fomites
	- Vectors

- Transmission via vectors
	- Vectors are organisms that feed on infected animals and transmit virus through their bite
	- Most vectors are anthorpods (insects, mites, ticks)
	- Arboviruses
	- Transmission within seconds or minutes after acquiring virus
		- Stuck to mouth parts
	- Transmission within hours or days after acquiring virus
		- Circulative transmission (git, blood, saliva)
	- Some viruses can have vector-vector transmissions
		- Sexual and transovarial (via egg)

- Zika virus
	- Aedes mosquito
	- Passed from a pregnant woman to her ffetus
		- Causing microcephaly
	- No vaccine or medicine
	- Cases found in 89 countries
	- Relatively rare
		- 27,000 cases (2023)

- Non vector transmission
	- Mucous membranes
		- Upper respiratory tract (SARS-CoV2)
		- Genital secretions (HIV)
		- Intestinal secretions via feces (Polio)
	- Lesions
		- Feet (foot and mouth disease)
		- Hands (HPV)
		- Lips (Herpes)
	- Blood
		- Animal bite (rabies)

# Mechanisms of viral entry

- Preferred routes of entry
	- Enter hosts through skin or mucous membranes
		- Respiratory tract
		- Gastrointestinal tract
		- Genital track
		- Conjunctiva (mucous membrane lining the eyes and eyelids)
		- Crossing the placenta (bloodborne viruses)

- Respiratory tract entry
	- Microbes inhaled daily
		- 8 per minutes
	- Defenses against viruses
		- Coughed out/swallowed
		- Macrophages
		- Antibodies
	- When immune system comprised of ineffective
		- Viruses attach to the receptors on the ciliated epithelial cells on the respiratory tract
		- Adenoviruses, Influenza A, Rhinoviruses

- Gastrointestinal Tract Entry
	- Viruses enter through oral fecal route
		- Feces enter through mouth, enter gastrointestinal tract
	- First defense
		- Acid and proteolytic enzymes in the stomach deactivate viruses
		- Viruses resistant to acid and bile enter the gastrointestinal tract
	- Stomach to intestine (enteric viruses)
	- Successful infection
		- Enteric viruses attach to the intestinal epithelial cells
	- Rotavirus, Norovirus

- Conjunctiva Entry

		- Uveitis and Ebola virus

- Other routes of entry
	- Skin entry
		- Outer layer of skin is dead cells
		- Viruses require metabolically active cells and must enter through breaks in skin
			- Bite
			- Needle-stick injuries
			- Body piercing
			- Biting insects

- Rabies
	- The majority of rabies cases are due to bat bites
		- Silver haired bast
		- Mexican free tailed bat
	- Muscle cells replication
	- Migrates to the CNS
	- Difficulty swallowing, hydrophobia, and tremors
		- Universally fatal

# Pathogenesis

- Mechanisms of viral spread or pathogenesis
	- Replication and infection within the host
		- Localized infections
			- At or near the site of entry
		- Systemic infections
			- Spread to other organs via bloodstream or CNS

- Localized viral infections
	- Concentrated in one part of the body are are not spread to other locations in the body
		- Papillomaviruses that cause skin warts
		- Rhinoviruses and adenoviruses
		- Noroviruses and rotaviruses

- System viral infections
	- Spread via bloodstream and peripheral nervous system
	- Primary viremia
		- The initial spread of virus in the blood from the first site of infection
	- Secondary viremia
		- When primary viremia has resulted in infection of additional tissues via bloodstream, in which the virus has replicated and once more entered the circulation
	- Entervirus
	- Herpes simplex virus 1
	- Herpes simplex virus 2

- Mechanisms of viral spread or pathogenesis
	- Target organs
		- Skin
			- Rashes are caused by viruses that have invaded the skin through the blood vessels
				- Macules
					- Patches of skin that are discoloured but not elevated
				- Papules
					- Small, solid, inflammatory elevations
				- Vesicles
					- Blisters on the skin that contain fluid
				- Pustules
					- Elevations on the skin with pus
		- Lungs and liver
			- Lungs
				- Respiratory tract
			- Liver
				- Hepatitis virus
		- Kidneys and heart
			- Infected by only a few viruses
			- CMV and hantaviruses
			- Coxsackie B (heart)
		- CNS
			- Neurotropic viruses infect nervous system
			- Rabies, poliovirus
- Viral infections and pregnancy
	- Transplacental, perinatal, postnatal transmission
	- Severe infections
		- Spontaneous abortion, stillbirth, and neonatal death
	- Less lethal viruses
		- Congenital malformations (Zika)

# Patterns of Disease

- Four patterns of viral infection
	- Acute infection
		- Sudden onset, last a short time
		- Common cold (adenoviruses)
	- Acute infection followed by persistent latent infection
		- DNA viruses
			- Herpes viruses
		- Acute onset, latent infection is dormant until reactivated into a productive infection
	- Chronic infection with continuous shedding of virus
		- Continuous production of viruses that last more than 6 months
		- Hepatitis B C
		- Chronic liver inflammation
		- Cirrhosis of the liver and liver cancer
	- Slow infection
		- Long incubation periods that lead to slow, progressive diseases
		- HIV and prions

- Virus Exit: Shedding
	- Shedding of human viruses
		- Usually shed from their routes of entry
		- Viruses infecting brain are shed through other organs, which are the primary site of replication
	- Diseases spread from animals to humans (zoonosis)
		- Urine and droppings (hantaviruses)
		- Respiratory droplets (Influenza)
		- Bushmeat a major source of zoonosis
	- Emerging pathogens
		- Hantaviruses
		- Arena-viruses
		- SARS
		- MERS
		- Rabies
		- Ebola

- Survival of Viruses in the environment
	- Factors affecting virus survival
		- Composition of the virus
		- Virus present in human and animal wastes
		- Temperature
		- Humidity
		- pH

# Human viruses in water environments

- Humans exposed to enteric viruses through
	- Consumption of undercooked shellfish grown in contaminated waters
	- Food crops using wastewater for irrigation or human sewage as fertilizer
	- Sewage-polluted recreational and drinking water
- Viruses more stable in water than bacteria

# Epidemiology

- The study of that which befalls man
- The study of how diseases affect whole communities
- Determine the distribution, cause, control and prevention of diseases in populations
- Describing disease events
- Progression of disease or infection
- Host and pathogen interactions
- Disease agents

- Describing disease events
	- Endemic
		- Disease that persists at a moderate and steady level within a geographic area
	- Sporadic
		- Disease outbreak that have no pattern of occurrence in a given time or location
	- Epidemic
		- An unusually high number of cases in excess of normal expectations
	- Pandemic
		- A worldwide epidemic
	- Etiological event
		- The pathogen
		- Disease causing agent

# Describing disease events

- Incidence
	- Measurement of morbility
	- Number of new causes of a disease that occurs in a specified period of time in a susceptible population
- Prevalence
	- Measurement of morbidity
	- Number of cases existing in a population at a specific time
- Morbidity
	- Illness of cases of a particular disease
	- Incidence per a population
	- Death caused by a particular disease
- Morbidity and mortality
	- Allow of evaluation of the efficacy of a health care system or implemented intervention
	- Assess burdens that a health event may place on a population
	- Allow stakeholders to prioritize which health events to tackle and allocate resources toward and proactively manage the potential onset of a health event
- Etiological agent or pathogen
	- Disease causing agent
	- HIV is the etiological agent of acquired immune deficiency syndrome (AIDS)

# Progression of disease or infection

- Case definition
	- A standardized set of criteria used to identify individuals with a particular disease during an outbreak investigation.
		- Clinical information
		- Demographic information
		- Location of the outbreak
		- A specified time period of disease
	- Course of infection
		- Incubation period
			- Time between infection with a virus and onset of symptoms
		- Prodromal period
			- First appearance of mild or nonspecific signs and symptoms of an illness
		- Communicable period
			- Time period when an infected individual or animal is contagious and can directly or indirectly transmit to.infect another person, animal, or arthropod
		- Convalescence
			- The recovery period after a disease

# Host and Pathogen Interactions

- Modes of transmission
	- How infectious disease is spread or passed on (direct or indirect)
	- Types of disease carriers
		- True carrier
			- Harbours the pathogen, but does not get sick
		- Incubatory carrier
			- An infected person during the incubation period of an infectious disease who is capable of spreading the pathogen
		- Convalescent carrier
			- People who remain infectious during and after their recovery from illness

# Progression of Disease or Infection

- Infectious dose
	- The minimum number of infectious agents or amount of infectious material necessary to establish infection and produce disease
- 1918 pandemic influenza A virus = 1-10 virions
- Norovirus = 10-100 virions
- Seasonal influenza = 1000-2000
- SARS-COV-2 = unknown
	- Animal studies = 36-1000 virions

- Virulence
	- A measure of severity of disease caused by a pathogen
- R-naught ($R_0$)
	- A measure of the potential for transmission or the reproduction rate of an infectious disease
	- Number of people that one sick person will infect (on average)

# Prevention and Containment of Contagious Diseases

- Quarantine
	- A segregation of healthy persons who are not ill but have been exposed to an individual who suffers from a communicable disease
- Isolation
	- The separation of ill.infected and contagious individuals from healthy individuals
