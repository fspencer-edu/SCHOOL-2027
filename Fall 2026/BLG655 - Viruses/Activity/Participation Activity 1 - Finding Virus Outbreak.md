## Post

A recent outbreak of Bundibugyo virus disease, a severe form of Ebola, was reported by the World Health Organization (WHO) on September 10, 2026. The outbreak has affected the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) and has spread to new areas, including Kayna in North Kivu. As of September 7, there were 6,757 confirmed cases and 3,267 deaths, resulting in a case fatality rate of about 48%. Cases have been reported in 61 health zones (local public health areas used to organize healthcare and disease monitoring) across six provinces, with Ituri being the most affected area.

Bundibugyo virus disease can spread through contact with an infected person’s body fluids or contaminated items. It can also originate in animals, with fruit bats believed to be a possible source. Spread between people is more likely during close contact, including within households, medical facilities, and certain local funeral practices.

Controlling the outbreak has been difficult because of delayed case detection, limited healthcare access, displacement, and conflict in affected areas. These challenges slowed testing, treatment, and contact tracing. WHO and local health authorities are continuing to work to track cases, improve testing, reduce transmission, and support safer care and burial practices. The risk within the DRC is considered very high, while the global risk remains low.
