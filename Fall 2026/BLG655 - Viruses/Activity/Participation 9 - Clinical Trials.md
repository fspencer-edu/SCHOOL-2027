The virus I chose to investigate for this assignment is measles, a highly contagious viral infection that can cause serious complications, particularly in young children. One of the main preventative measures against measles is the measles, mumps, and rubella (MMR) vaccine. Klein et al. (2020) conducted a Phase III clinical trial involving 5,003 children aged 12–15 months to evaluate the safety and immune response of the Priorix vaccine compared with M-M-R II. The study found that over 97% of participants developed measurable immune responses, with both vaccines showing similar safety profiles. Additional trials reported consistent findings, including a Phase II study showing measles antibody response rates of 98.3–99.2% (Mufson et al., 2015) and a Phase III study involving children aged 4–6 years confirming comparable immune responses after a second dose (MMR-158 Study Group, 2019). Overall, these studies support the safety and immunogenicity of Priorix as a preventative measure against measles, although they measured antibody responses rather than actual measles infection rates.
