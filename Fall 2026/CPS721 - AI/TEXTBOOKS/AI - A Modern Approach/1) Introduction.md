## The Laws of Thought Approach

	- Aristotle
	- Irrefutable reasoning processes
	- Patterns for argument structures that always yields a correct conclusions given the correct premises

- Probability
	- Rigorous reasoning with uncertain information
	- Comprehensive model of rational thought from raw perceptual information
	- Does not generate intelligent behaviour

## The Rational Agent Approach

- Agent
	- Something that acts
	- Operate autonomously, perceive their environment, persist over a prolonged time period, adapt to change, and create and pursue goals
- Rational agent
	- Acts to achieve the best outcome when there is uncertainty
	- Deduce that a given action is best and then act on that conclusion
	- More general that the "laws of thought"
		- Correct inference is one of the several possible mechanisms for rationality
	- Amenable to scientific development
- Control theory/standard model
	- A controller minimizes a cost function
	- Policy maximizes a sum of rewards
	- Rule minimizes a loss function
	- Decision maker maximizes utility of some measure
- Limited rationality
	- Acting when there is not enough time to do all the computations

## Philosophy

- Aristotle (384-322 BCE)
	- Formulate a precise set of laws governing the rational mind
	- Syllogism
- Ramon Llull (1232-1215)
	- The Great Art
	- System of reasoning
- Rene Decartes (1596-1650)
	- Mind and matter
	- Physical conception of the mind
	- Proponent of dualism
	- Part of the mind outside of nature

- Materialism
	- Brain operates according to the laws of physics
	- Physicalism
	- Naturalism
- Empiricism
	- The view that knowledge comes primarily from observation and experience
- Induction
	- Reasoning from specific observations to a general conclusion or rule
- Logical positivism
	- All knowledge can be characterized by logical theories connected, to observation sentences that corresponds to sensory inputs
	- Rationalism and empiricism
- Confirmation theory
	- The study of how evidence supports or increases confidence in a hypothesis or theory
- General Problem Solver
	- An early AI system that solves problems using means–ends analysis, repeatedly reducing the difference between the current state and the goal state
- Greedy regression planning system
	- A planning approach that works backward from the goal, choosing actions that appear to reduce the remaining goals most directly at each step
- Utility
	- Subjective value of an outcome
	- Maximizing expected utility
- Utilitarianism
	- Rational decision making based on maximizing utility should apply to all spheres of human activity
- Consequentialism
	- Right and wrong is determined by expected outcomes of an action
- Deontological ethics
	- The right thing is not determined by outcomes, but by universal social laws that govern allowable actions

## Mathematics

	- Gödel’s result that any sufficiently powerful consistent formal system contains some true statements that cannot be proven within that system

## Economics

- Decision theory
	- A framework for choosing the best action under uncertainty by combining probabilities with utilities/preferences
- Game theory
	- The study of decision-making when multiple rational agents interact, where each agent’s outcome depends on others’ actions
	- Randomized strategies can sometimes be optimal
- Multiagent systems
	- Systems containing multiple autonomous agents that interact, cooperate, or compete to achieve goals
- Operations research
	- The use of mathematical models, optimization, and data analysis to make better decisions about complex systems, such as scheduling, routing, resource allocation, and logistics
	- Sequential decision problems called Markov decision processes
- Markov Decision Process (MDP)
	- A mathematical model for sequential decision-making under uncertainty, where an agent chooses actions, receives rewards, and moves between states based on probabilities
	- Reinforcement learning
- Satisficing
	- Choosing a solution that is good enough to meet a required threshold, rather than searching for the absolute optimal solution

# The History of AI

## The inception of AI (1943-1956)

- Knowledge of the basic physiology and function of neurons in the brain
- A formal analysis of propositional logic
- Theory of computation

- Hebbian learning
	- A learning rule where connections between neurons become stronger when the neurons are activated together
- Each neuron is characterized as being on or off
## Early enthusiasm, great expectations (1952-1969)

- Physical symbol system
	- A physical symbol system has the necessary and sufficient means for general intelligent action
- Resolution method
	- A complete theorem proving algorithm for first order logic
- Micro-worlds (Minsky/MIT)
	- Simplified artificial environments used in early AI research to study intelligence in a small, controlled world with limited objects and rules
- Blocks world
	- Consists of a set of solid blocks placed on a tabletop
	- Rearrange the blocks one at a time
	- Visual and constraint propagation
	- Learning theory
	- Natural language understanding program
	- Planner
- Adalines
- Perceptrons
- Perceptron convergence theorem
	- The learning algorithm can adjust the connection strengths of a perceptron to match any input data, provided such a match exists

## A dose of reality (1966-1973)

- Many early AI systems were based on "inform introspection"
- Lack of appreciation of the intractability of many of the problems that AI was attempting to solve
- Machine evolution/generic programming
	- Making an appropriate series of small mutations to machine code program, can generate a program with good performance for any particular task
	- No progress
- Perceptrons represented very little
- Two-input perceptron could not be trained to recognize when its two inputs were different
	- Multi-layer networks
	- Neural net

## Expert Systems (1969-1986)

- Weak methods
	- General purpose search mechanism
	- Do not scale up to large or difficult problem instances
- Expert systems
	- Domain specific knowledge
	- Narrow areas of expertise
	- Heuristic Programming Project (HPP)
- Certainty factors
	- Calculus of uncertainty
- Frames
	- Assembling facts about particular object and event types
	- Arranging the types into a large taxonomic hierarchy

## The return of neural networks (1986-present)

- Back propagation
	- A training algorithm for neural networks that propagates prediction errors backward through the network to update weights
- Connectionist
	- An AI approach that models intelligence using networks of interconnected simple units, inspired by neurons; modern neural networks are connectionist systems
